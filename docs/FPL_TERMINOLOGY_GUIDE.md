# FPL Terminology Guide

> **For new FPL managers using Manny's FPL House.**
> The Assistant Manager speaks fluent FPL. This guide teaches you the language.

---

## Welcome

The Assistant Manager in Manny's FPL House is built for experienced Fantasy
Premier League players. It uses FPL shorthand, community terminology, statistical
language, and model-specific jargon naturally -- the way real FPL managers talk
to each other.

This is intentional. We do not want to dumb it down.

If you understand football but are new to FPL, this guide will teach you what
the Assistant Manager is saying. If you are an experienced FPL player, you
probably already know most of this -- and the Assistant Manager already speaks
your language.

---

## 1. The Basics

These are the core building blocks of Fantasy Premier League.

### GK / GKP -- Goalkeeper

The goalkeeper position. In FPL you must carry exactly 2 goalkeepers in your
squad, and exactly 1 starts.

### DEF -- Defender

Defenders. You carry up to 5 defenders and start at least 3. Defenders earn
4 points for a clean sheet (the team conceding no goals while they are on the
pitch).

### MID -- Midfielder

Midfielders. You carry up to 5 and start at least 2. Midfielders earn 5 points
per goal and 1 point for a clean sheet.

### FWD -- Forward

Forwards. You carry up to 3 and start at least 1. Forwards earn 4 points per
goal.

### XI

The starting 11 players -- your actual playing team for the gameweek. The
remaining 4 players are on the bench.

### Squad

Your full 15-player roster. FPL rules require exactly 15 players: 2 GKP, 5 DEF,
5 MID, 3 FWD.

### Formation

The arrangement of your starting outfield players (the goalkeeper is always
1). Common formations the Assistant Manager references: 3-5-2, 4-4-2, 4-5-1,
3-4-3, 5-3-2, etc. The numbers refer to DEF-MID-FWD.

> **Example:** "Your best formation this week is 3-5-2."
> Translation: Start 1 GK, 3 defenders, 5 midfielders, 2 forwards.

## 2. Gameweeks & Fixtures

### Gameweek (GW)

A round of Premier League fixtures. The 2026-27 season has 38 gameweeks.
Every player earns or loses FPL points based on their real-world performance
in that gameweek.

### Fixture

A specific match a player's team has in a gameweek. The Assistant Manager
shows fixtures like `GW5 BOU (H)` meaning "in gameweek 5, play Bournemouth
at home" or `GW5 LIV (A)` meaning "away at Liverpool."

### H / A -- Home / Away

Whether a fixture is at the player's home ground (H) or away (A). Home
fixtures are generally considered easier.

### Difficulty Rating (FDR / diff)

A 1-5 scale the official FPL game assigns to each fixture. 1 = very easy,
3 = neutral, 5 = very hard. The Assistant Manager often shows this as
`diff 2` or `diff 4`.

### DGW -- Double Gameweek

A gameweek where a team plays twice (two fixtures). Players from that
team get points from both matches. Double gameweeks are strategically
important -- chips like Bench Boost and Triple Captain are often saved
for DGWs.

### BGW -- Blank Gameweek

A gameweek where a team does NOT play (their fixture is postponed).
Players from blanked teams score zero that week. The Free Hit chip is
often used to navigate BGWs.

### Fixture Swing / Fixture Run

A period of several gameweeks where a team's fixtures become notably
easier or harder. The Assistant Manager detects these to recommend
transfers.

> **Example:** "Fixtures improve by +1.5 over the next 3 GWs."
> Translation: The upcoming opponents are significantly easier than
> the recent ones.

## 3. Squad Structure & Budget

### Budget / £100m

Every FPL manager starts with a virtual budget of 100.0m to buy 15
players. Prices are in millions (e.g. "Haaland costs 15.5m").

### Bank / ITB -- In The Bank

The money left over after buying your squad. If your 15 players cost
99.5m, your bank is 0.5m. ITB is the abbreviation for "In The Bank."
The Assistant Manager uses both terms interchangeably.

### Price / Cost

What a player costs. Written as £Xm (e.g. "Saka is 9.5m"). Prices
change during the season based on transfer market activity.

### Price Rise / Price Drop

Player prices go up or down based on how many managers are buying or
selling them. A rise costs you more if you buy later; a drop loses you
value if you already own them. The Assistant Manager tracks price
movements to warn you.

### Value Score

A Manny's FPL House metric (0-100) measuring how much points-per-million
a player delivers. Higher = better value for money.

### Structure / Team Structure

How your budget is distributed across positions. A "heavy midfield"
structure spends more on midfielders; a "template" structure follows
what most managers are doing.

> **Example:** "Your team is heavily concentrated in midfield -- Bruno
> (12.0m) and Haaland (15.5m) eat up 27.5% of budget."
> Translation: Two expensive players leave little room elsewhere.

## 4. Transfers

### Transfer

Selling one player and buying another. You get 1 free transfer per
gameweek. Additional transfers cost 4 points each (a "hit").

### Free Transfer (FT)

The one transfer you get each week at no points cost. If you don't use
it, you can carry one over (up to a maximum of 5 saved).

### Hit / -4

Losing 4 FPL points for making an extra transfer beyond your free
transfers. A -4 hit means you used 2 transfers (1 free + 1 hit). A -8
means 3 transfers. The Assistant Manager evaluates whether the expected
points gain justifies the hit.

> **Example:** "A -4 hit is justified. Expected gain of +7.2 points
> outweighs the 4-point cost."
> Translation: You should take the hit because you'll gain more than
> you lose.

### Roll / Rolling a Transfer

Not using your free transfer this week so you carry 2 into next week.
You can only roll once (max 5 saved).

### Wildcard (WC)

A chip that lets you make unlimited free transfers for one gameweek.
You get 2 wildcards per season. Often used for major squad rebuilds.

### Free Hit (FH)

A chip that lets you make unlimited transfers for one gameweek, but
your squad reverts to its original state afterwards. Useful for
navigating blank or double gameweeks.

### Sell / Buy / Bring In / Sign / Swap / Replace

The Assistant Manager uses all of these interchangeably to describe
transfers. "Sell Wilson, bring in Saka" = trade Wilson for Saka.

### Downgrade / Upgrade

Selling an expensive player and buying a cheaper one (downgrade) or
vice versa (upgrade). Downgrades free up money; upgrades improve a
position.

### Hold

Keeping a player rather than selling them. "Recommendation: Hold" means
no transfer is advised this week.

> **Example:** "No transfer recommended. Your squad is well-positioned
> for the upcoming gameweeks."
> Translation: Don't waste a transfer -- your team is fine.

## 5. Player Types

### Premium

The most expensive, high-scoring players. Typically 10.0m+. Examples:
Haaland, Salah, Saka, Bruno Fernandes. Premium players are expected to
deliver big points regularly.

### Mid-Price

Players in the 6.0m-8.5m range. Solid starters who aren't the most
expensive but offer good returns.

### Budget

Cheap starters, typically 4.5m-5.5m. They're not going to top the
charts, but they play regularly and earn steady points.

### Enabler

A cheap player whose main purpose is to free up funds for expensive
players elsewhere. A 4.0m defender who starts is an enabler -- they let
you afford Haaland.

### Fodder / Bench Fodder

The cheapest players in your squad who you never intend to start. Their
sole purpose is to fill roster spots at minimum cost. A 4.0m goalkeeper
who never plays is bench fodder.

The official FPL glossary defines bench fodder as: "A member of your
squad typically costing little money, such as a 4.0m goalkeeper or 4.0m
defender, who you intend to rarely or never use in your starting 11."

> **Example (Assistant Manager):** "Burrowes (4.5m) is pure fodder --
> 0.00 xPts this week."
> Translation: He's only in the squad to save money, not to score.

### Differential

A player with low ownership (typically under 10%) who can differentiate
your team from competitors. If a differential scores big while few
managers own him, you gain significant rank.

> **Example (Assistant Manager):** "Differential pick: Mac Allister,
> 1.7% owned, 6.6 xPts."
> Translation: Almost nobody has this player, so if he scores, you
> leapfrog many rivals.

### Template

The collection of most-highly-owned players. A "template team" is one
built from popular picks. Following the template is safe; deviating
requires conviction.

The template shifts throughout the season as new value picks emerge.

### Punt

A risky, unconventional pick. Often a player who is slightly doubtful
or unproven but has upside. "Taking a punt" means gambling on a
high-risk, high-reward option.

### Essential

A player the FPL community considers a must-have. If you don't own an
essential player and they score, you lose ground to almost everyone.

## 6. Minutes & Playing Time

### Nailed / Nailed On

A player who is considered almost certain to start. "Nailed" means the
manager trusts them and they start week in, week out. Not an official
FPL designation -- this is community shorthand.

> **Example (Assistant Manager):** "He's nailed and has strong xGI/90."
> Translation: The model believes he starts consistently AND contributes
> goals/assists at a high rate.

### Rotation Risk

A player who might not start due to squad rotation -- the manager
resting players across competitions. High rotation risk means the
player could be benched unexpectedly.

> **Example:** "Rotation risk (450 mins so far)."
> Translation: He hasn't accumulated many minutes, suggesting he may
> not be a guaranteed starter.

### Minutes Risk

Similar to rotation risk. The player's expected minutes are uncertain
due to competition for their position, injury recovery, or fixture
congestion.

### Expected Minutes

A Manny's FPL House metric. The model's probability-weighted estimate
of how many minutes a player will play. Calculated as:

`expected_minutes = start_probability x minutes_if_starting x (1 - substitution_risk)`

### Start Probability

The probability (0-100%) that a player starts the next match. Based on
historical starts, FPL status data, and form. Shown as a percentage
in the Assistant Manager's player context.

### Substitution Risk

The probability of being subbed off before 60 minutes (which affects
bonus points and clean sheet chances). The model estimates this based
on the player's historical substitution patterns.

### Starter

A player who starts in the starting XI (as opposed to coming on as a
substitute or being benched).

### Bench Player

A player not in the starting XI for a given gameweek. Can be a
deliberate strategy (bench fodder) or an unwanted situation (a quality
player unexpectedly rested).

### Reliable Minutes

A player who consistently plays full matches (80-90 minutes). Valued
because they accumulate more chances to score points.

> **Example:** "Minimum-cost Reliable Minutes" is a Manny's FPL House
> concept -- the cheapest players who still reliably start and play
> significant minutes.

## 7. Points & Performance

### Return / Attacking Return

When a player records a goal, assist, or other points-generating event.
A "return" in FPL means the player earned points beyond the baseline
for just playing. Clean sheets and penalty saves also count as returns.

### Blank

When a player scores minimal points -- typically 2 points or fewer for
a start, or 0-1 for a substitute appearance. "He blanked" means he
didn't produce.

### Haul

A big points score in a single gameweek -- typically 10+ points.
"Hauling" means a player had an exceptional performance.

> **Example:** "Haaland hauled 18 points in GW3."
> Translation: Haaland scored a massive 18-point gameweek.

### Clean Sheet (CS)

The team concedes no goals while the player is on the pitch. Defenders
and goalkeepers earn 4 points; midfielders earn 1 point. Forwards get
nothing for clean sheets.

### Bonus Points

Extra points (1-3) awarded to the top 3 performers in each match based
on the Bonus Points System (BPS). The best performer gets 3, second
gets 2, third gets 1.

### Ceiling

The maximum realistic points a player could score in a gameweek. A
premium striker in great form with a easy fixture has a high ceiling.

### Floor

The minimum realistic points a player could score. A nailed defender
on a strong team has a relatively high floor -- even on a bad day they
get 2 points for playing.

### Upside

The potential for a big score. High-upside players are capable of
hauls but may also blank. "Chasing upside" means prioritizing
ceiling over safety.

> **Example:** "Palmer is on penalties and will have a great run
> coming up -- he has massive upside."
> Translation: He could score very big points, making the risk
> worthwhile.

## 8. Statistics & Analytics

### xG -- Expected Goals

A statistical measure of the quality of goal-scoring chances. Each
shot is assigned a probability of being scored (0 to 1) based on
distance, angle, defender proximity, etc. A player with 0.80 xG in a
match was "expected" to score roughly 1 goal from their chances.

Official FPL glossary: "A statistic that measures the quality of the
goalscoring opportunities presented to a player, based on how often
similar chances result in a goal."

### xA -- Expected Assists

A statistical measure of the likelihood that a pass becomes a goal
assist. Highlights players who create dangerous chances, even if
the finisher missed.

Official FPL glossary: "A statistic that measures the number of
standard assists that a player was expected to have returned."

### xGI -- Expected Goal Involvement

The sum of xG + xA. Measures overall attacking threat. A player with
0.60 xGI/90 is heavily involved in goals.

Official FPL glossary: "A statistic that measures both the number of
goals and standard assists that a team or player were expected to
return."

### xGI/90 -- xGI per 90 minutes

The expected goal involvement rate normalized per 90 minutes of play.
Allows comparison between players regardless of how many minutes
they've played. The Assistant Manager flags thresholds:

- **Excellent:** xGI/90 >= 0.50
- **Good:** 0.25-0.49
- **Low:** < 0.10 (for MID/FWD -- flagged as concern)

> **Example (Assistant Manager):** "Excellent xGI/90 (0.58)."
> Translation: This player is creating or scoring goals at an elite
> rate every time they play.

### xPts -- Expected Fantasy Points

A Manny's FPL House term. The V3 model's projection of how many FPL
points a player will score in the next gameweek. This is the core
output of the platform's prediction model.

### xPts/90 -- Expected Points per 90 Minutes

The projected points rate per full match. Allows comparison of
players regardless of expected minutes. A player with 0.80 xPts/90
who starts is projected for more points than one with 0.40 xPts/90.

### xGC / xGA -- Expected Goals Conceded

The number of goals a team is expected to concede. Used to evaluate
defenders and goalkeepers for clean sheet potential. Lower = better
for defensive assets.

### Form

FPL's official rolling average of recent points scored. A player with
form 7.0 has averaged 7 points per gameweek recently. The Assistant
Manager uses form as a quick snapshot of recent performance.

### Points Per 90 (PP90)

Actual points scored per 90 minutes of play. A pure historical
performance metric (unlike xPts which is forward-looking).

### Underlying Numbers / Underlying Stats

The raw statistical data (xG, xA, xGI, BPS, shots, key passes) that
form the basis for predictions. "Looking at the underlying numbers"
means examining whether a player's performance is sustainable.

### ICT Index

Official FPL metric: Influence, Creativity, Threat. A composite index
ranking players on their overall attacking contribution. Available on
the official FPL site.

### BPS -- Bonus Points System

The system that determines bonus points after each match. Based on
events like tackles, interceptions, clearances, key passes, chances
created, goals, assists. The top 3 BPS scorers get 3/2/1 bonus points.

## 9. Captaincy

### Captain / (C)

The player you designate as captain. Their points are doubled for the
gameweek. Captaincy is one of the most impactful decisions each week.

> **Example:** "B.Fernandes (C)" = Bruno Fernandes is captain.

### Vice-Captain (VC)

The player who becomes captain if your designated captain doesn't play
at all. Their points are NOT doubled -- they only become captain as a
backup.

### Captaincy Option / Captain Candidate

A player being considered for the captain's armband. The Assistant
Manager ranks captain picks by V3 xPts.

### Effective Ownership (EO)

The percentage of teams starting a player, weighted by captaincy. If
100% of managers own Haaland and 50% captain him, his EO is 150%. If
you own him but don't captain him while most others do, his points
hurt your rank relative to them.

### Armband

Colloquial term for the captaincy. "Wearing the armband" = being
captain. The Assistant Manager recognizes "armband" as an intent
word for captaincy queries.

### Differential Captain

A captain pick with low ownership. Risky but high reward -- if your
low-owned captain hauls while the popular captain blanks, you gain
massive rank.

## 10. The Bench

### Bench

The 4 players not in your starting XI. They don't score points unless
the Bench Boost chip is active, or unless a starting player doesn't
play (in which case the first substitute auto-substitutes in).

### Bench Boost (BB / bboost)

A chip that adds your bench players' points to the gameweek total.
Typically played during a Double Gameweek when bench players also have
two fixtures.

### Bench Cover

A substitute who is likely to come on if a starter doesn't play. A
good bench has cover across positions so auto-substitutions work.

### Playing Bench / Playable Bench

A bench where the substitutes are regular starters for their teams --
meaning they'll get meaningful minutes even as subs. Better than
having pure fodder on the bench.

> **Example:** "Bench is performing well (avg form 3.2). Consider
> Bench Boost when a DGW arrives."
> Translation: Your substitutes are scoring decent points, so the
> Bench Boost chip would add meaningful points.

## 11. Chips & Strategy

### Chip

A one-time-use power-up. You get 4 chips total per season (2 of each):
Wildcard, Free Hit, Triple Captain, Bench Boost. First batch expires
after GW19; second batch available from GW20 onward.

### Triple Captain (TC / 3xc)

A chip that triples your captain's points for one gameweek instead of
doubling them. Best used during a DGW when your captain plays twice.

### Set and Forget

A strategy where you pick a player (especially a goalkeeper or
captain) and never change them. The opposite of actively managing
each week.

### Dead-Ending

Targeting short-term fixture picks who you plan to sell on your
wildcard. "Dead-ending into a wildcard" means filling your squad with
short-term punts before a major rebuild.

## 12. Assistant Manager Terminology

These terms are specific to Manny's FPL House and the Assistant Manager's
analytical framework. You will not find them in the official FPL glossary.

### V3

The production prediction model. When the Assistant Manager says
"V3 projects..." or "V3 says...", it is referring to the platform's
primary expected points model. V3 is the authoritative source for all
numbers.

### Model D / Shadow Model

An alternative prediction model running alongside V3 as a control
group. Model D (internal ID: V3-HIST-01) uses a different historical
calibration approach. It is shown for comparison when the user asks
about model disagreement. The Assistant Manager will say "Model D
agrees" or "Model D disagrees."

### Shadow Projections

The output from non-primary models (V1, V2, Model D) that run in
parallel with V3. Shadow models are never promoted automatically --
they must pass sustained validation before replacing V3.

### Start Probability

The model's estimate (0-100%) of whether a player starts. Shown in
player context as a percentage. Low start probability = rotation risk.

> **Example (Assistant Manager):** "*Caution: Saka start probability
> is 85% this week.*"
> Translation: There's a 15% chance he doesn't start -- consider
> alternatives.

### Expected Minutes

The model's probability-weighted estimate of minutes played. More
precise than just "expected to start" because it accounts for
substitution risk and historical patterns.

### Confidence / Confidence Score

A 0-100 measure of how certain the model is about its projection.
High confidence = the model has strong data backing its forecast.
Low confidence = the projection is more uncertain.

### Data Quality Tier

How much historical data the model has for a player: "none",
"limited", "moderate", or "good". A new signing has "limited" data;
a long-tenured Premier League player has "good."

### Contributing Factors

The explainability metadata behind each projection. Shows which
inputs (minutes, xGI, fixtures, set pieces, form) drove the xPts
number. The Assistant Manager can show these on request.

### Provenance

The source origin of any number the Assistant Manager states. Every
number is labeled:

- **"V3 projects..."** -- model output
- **"FPL data shows..."** -- raw FPL API data
- **"Based on your assumption..."** -- user-provided information
- **"That suggests..."** -- the assistant's own inference

### Net Expected Gain

The projected points improvement from a transfer minus any hit cost.
"Net expected gain: +3.8 points" means the model expects you to gain
3.8 points over the gameweek after accounting for the -4 hit.

### Rotation Risk Label

The model's qualitative assessment of start certainty: "Low" (nearly
certain to start), "Medium" (possible rotation), "High" (significant
doubt).

### Feature Store

The central data layer that feeds both V3 engines. It is the single
source of truth for all inputs -- no engine recomputes features.

### Evidence Layer

An optional research/ablation framework that can override V3's default
inputs with calibrated historical data. In production, the evidence
layer is NOT active -- it is only used in controlled experiments.

### Historical Calibration (Hist Configs)

Empirical scaling factors derived from past seasons that improve V3's
accuracy. Examples: finishing multipliers, clean sheet probability
curves, team strength adjustments.

## 14. Why Does the Assistant Manager Talk Like This?

The Assistant Manager intentionally uses established FPL community language
rather than translating every concept into formal language. This is a
deliberate design choice.

**The reasoning:** Experienced FPL managers think and communicate in this
shorthand. When they ask "Who should I captain this week?" or "Can I afford
Haaland?", they expect the system to understand and respond in kind. The
Assistant Manager's system prompt (`services/assistant_chat/engine.py`)
explicitly instructs it to behave "like an experienced FPL analyst and
advisor."

**The result:** The Assistant Manager naturally uses terms like "xPts",
"fixture swing", "differential", "hit", "nailed", and "rotation risk"
without explanation -- the way an experienced friend at the pub would
talk about FPL.

**For new players:** This guide exists so you can learn the language
without the Assistant Manager having to slow down and explain itself
every time. The assistant stays fast and natural for experienced users;
you get a reference to look up unfamiliar terms.

This is the same approach used by professional FPL content creators,
podcasters, and the official FPL community. The language is the language
of FPL.

## Appendix C: Ambiguous or Non-Standard Terms

The following terms are used in the FPL community with varying definitions:

### "Nailed"
Not an official FPL designation. Generally means "almost certain to start"
but has no precise threshold. Different managers interpret it differently:
some mean 95%+ start probability, others mean 80%+. The Assistant Manager
uses the model's start probability percentage when precision matters.

### "Fodder" vs "Enabler"
These overlap significantly. The official FPL glossary distinguishes them:
- **Bench fodder**: "intended to rarely or never use in your starting 11"
- **Enabler**: "a cheap player who frees up funds"

In practice, the same player can be both. A 4.0m defender who occasionally
starts is simultaneously fodder (you don't plan to start them) and an
enabler (their low price lets you buy premiums).

### "xPts"
While "expected points" is a general concept in FPL analytics (fplform.com
uses "xP"), the specific "xPts" abbreviation and the V3 model producing it
are project-specific. Other FPL tools use different models and different
names for their projections.

### "Form"
FPL's official form stat is a simple rolling average. The Assistant Manager
uses it as one input among many. When the Assistant Manager says "strong
recent form," it is referencing FPL's official form number, not a
model-derived metric.

### "Value"
In FPL community usage, "value" typically means points-per-million or
price efficiency. The official FPL game displays a "value" stat. The
Assistant Manager's "Value Score" (0-100) is a project-specific
normalization of this concept.
